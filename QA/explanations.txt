PHASE 4 — RELIGION + WORK (pages 9-11) — CONCERNS FROM THE BRAINSTORM
========================================================================

These are the issues the audit found in the Phase 4 plan (PRD). For each
issue, I explain what the problem actually is in plain language, why it
matters, and what the choices are for fixing it.

Once you've read through them, tell me your choice for each and I'll
update the PRD accordingly.

ISSUE 1 — Screen name mismatch (BLOCKER)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT THE PLAN SAYS:
  Story 4.1 tells the developer to build a screen called
  "Page09ReligionScreen".

WHAT THE CODE ALREADY HAS:
  When Phase 2 built the navigation skeleton, it registered a placeholder
  called "Page09ReligionSubsectScreen" (note the extra word "Subsect").

WHY THIS MATTERS:
  Think of the app's navigation like a hotel. Every screen has a room
  number that the front desk uses to send guests to the right room.
  Right now, the front desk (navigation) has "Room 9 = Religion+Subsect"
  written on its map. If the developer builds a room called "Religion"
  (without "Subsect"), they have essentially built a new, unlisted room.
  The front desk will keep sending guests to the old, empty placeholder
  room, and the new room will sit unused. Or worse, the developer will
  add a second entry to the map, and now you have TWO rooms both trying
  to be page 9 — a duplicate.

  This is a naming drift bug. It's easy to fix but guarantees a broken
  wire-up if we don't catch it now.

YOUR CHOICES:
  A) Change the PRD to say "Page09ReligionSubsectScreen" (matches
     existing code — recommended).
  B) Change the code to rename the placeholder to "Page09ReligionScreen"
     (extra work in the navigator, no upside).

ISSUE 2 — Contradiction: "immutable" vs. "can be changed" (story 4.1)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT THE PLAN SAYS (two lines, both in story 4.1):
  Line A: "If the user picks Islam + subsect, then goes back and picks
           Christianity, the subsect must reset to 'Not Applicable'
           before advancing."
  Line B: "Both religion and subsect are treated as immutable-after-set
           in the draft."

WHY THESE CONTRADICT:
  "Immutable" means "cannot be changed once set."
  "Reset after back-nav" means "can be changed after set."

  You can't have both. It's like saying "this door is permanently locked"
  in one paragraph, and "when you come back through this door, unlock it
  first" in the next.

BACKGROUND ON WHY THE CODE CURRENTLY DOES SOMETHING SUBTLE:
  Phase 3 already added a helper called `isImmutable()`. Right now,
  religion and subsect are in that helper's "immutable list."
  BUT — and this is the important part — that list is NOT used to lock
  the draft. It is only consulted at the very end (Phase 11) when
  building the PATCH request to the server. At that point, if a field
  is "immutable," Phase 11 will refuse to send it to the server (because
  the server itself locks the field after the first save).

  In other words: the DRAFT (the in-memory form the user is filling out)
  is fully editable. The SERVER is where fields become frozen. The two
  live in different places.

WHY THIS MATTERS:
  If we don't clarify the plan, the developer might interpret "immutable
  in the draft" literally and add a lock that prevents back-navigation
  from editing religion. That would break the reset-to-Not-Applicable
  bullet. Or they might do the opposite. Either way, whichever they
  pick, half the acceptance criteria will fail their own test.

YOUR CHOICES:
  A) DRAFT-EDITABLE, SERVER-LOCKED (recommended, matches Phase 11 plan).
     Reword line B to: "religion + subsect participate in isImmutable()
     metadata used by Phase 11's PATCH-builder — no in-draft enforcement
     in Phase 4." Draft can be freely edited during onboarding.

  B) DRAFT-LOCKED. Delete the reset-after-back-nav bullet (line A) AND
     block back-navigation to page 9 once the user has moved past it.
     This is stricter but breaks the "user can review and edit their
     answers" pattern the wizard establishes elsewhere.

ISSUE 3 — Missing "where does the user go next?"
------------------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT THE PLAN SAYS:
  Story 4.1: "auto-advances" (to where? not stated)
  Story 4.2: "auto-advances" (to where? not stated)
  Story 4.3: correctly states "advance to Page12EducationLevelScreen"

WHY THIS MATTERS:
  Imagine giving a taxi driver the instruction "drive forward" without
  saying to which address. They'll pick a direction, and it might not
  be the one you meant. The developer will guess, and probably guess
  right (Page 9 → Page 10 → Page 11 is the obvious flow), but "probably"
  isn't good enough for acceptance criteria that need to be testable.

  A test needs a concrete expectation: "after tap, navigation.navigate
  was called with 'Page10ProfessionalCategoryScreen'." Vague acceptance
  criteria produce vague tests.

YOUR CHOICES:
  A) Fill in the blanks in the PRD (recommended):
       Story 4.1 → advances to Page10ProfessionalCategoryScreen
       Story 4.2 → advances to Page11WorkDetailsScreen
  B) Leave as-is, trust the developer to infer from context.

ISSUE 4 — Character rules reject real-world names (story 4.3)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT THE PLAN SAYS:
  For job title and employer name, allow only these characters:
  letters, digits, spaces, hyphen (-), period (.), comma (,).

WHY THIS IS TOO STRICT:
  A lot of real employers and job titles use characters this rule
  rejects. Examples of what would be blocked:

    - "P&G"                (rejected — & not allowed)
    - "Johnson & Johnson"  (rejected — & not allowed)
    - "Macy's"             (rejected — ' not allowed)
    - "O'Reilly Media"     (rejected — ' not allowed)
    - "AT&T"               (rejected — & not allowed)

  These are all legitimate employer names. If a user tries to type any
  of them, they'll hit a validation error and be unable to proceed.

  Note that Phase 3's `isValidName()` already allows apostrophes
  (needed for names like "O'Brien"). Being stricter on employer names
  than on person names doesn't make sense.

YOUR CHOICES:
  A) Add both & and ' to the allowed set (recommended, matches reality).
     Final rule: letters, digits, spaces, - . , & '
  B) Keep the rule as-is (users with those employers will be blocked).
  C) Loosen further to "any printable character" (fewer bugs, less
     protection against garbage input).

ISSUE 5 — Length limits aren't tied to the database
------------------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT THE PLAN SAYS:
  job_title max 40 characters
  employer_name max 50 characters
  office_address max 150 characters

WHY THIS MATTERS:
  These numbers came from somewhere, but the PRD doesn't say where.
  There's a file in the backend called `db-schema.json` that defines
  the "real" limits — how many characters each column in the database
  can actually hold.

  Think of it like a bucket. The database bucket might hold exactly
  50 characters for `employer_name`. If our form limits to 40, we're
  under-using the bucket (annoying for users with long employer names
  but harmless). If our form limits to 60, users will type 60 chars,
  hit Submit, and the database will reject or silently truncate the
  extra 10 characters — a bad-data bug.

  We need to confirm the numbers in the PRD (40, 50, 150) actually
  match the database schema. If they don't, we ship a subtle data
  corruption bug.

YOUR CHOICES:
  A) Have the developer verify against `knotify-backend/db-schema.json`
     during implementation and adjust if there's a mismatch
     (recommended — trivial to do, catches the bug).
  B) Trust the PRD numbers as-is (may ship a bug if wrong).
  C) You look up the db-schema numbers now and tell me what they are;
     I'll bake them into the PRD.

ISSUE 6 — Users won't know WHY the Continue button is disabled (4.3)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT THE PLAN SAYS:
  Page 11 has 5 fields. "Continue is disabled until all five fields
  validate."

WHY THIS ISN'T ENOUGH:
  Disabling a button tells the user "you can't proceed yet" but doesn't
  tell them "here's what's wrong."

  Imagine an ATM that says "TRANSACTION DENIED" without saying why —
  insufficient funds? Wrong PIN? Card expired? You'd tap around
  helplessly. Same thing here: user fills 4 fields, 5th field has a
  typo they can't see, Continue is grey, user is stuck.

  Page 2 (email/password) already established a pattern: each field
  shows its own inline error message ("password too short", "not a
  valid email", etc.). Page 11 should follow that pattern for
  consistency.

YOUR CHOICES:
  A) Require inline per-field error messages (recommended, matches
     Page 2 pattern). Each field shows its own error label.
  B) Just disable Continue and let the user figure it out (bad UX).

ISSUE 7 — Test doesn't catch changes to the options list (story 4.2)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT THE PLAN SAYS:
  "Renders all 27 entries." The test checks the count is 27.

WHY THIS IS BRITTLE:
  The 27 entries come from a JSON file (`professionalCategory.json`).
  If someone adds a 28th profession later, the file grows to 28 entries
  BUT the test still expects 27. The test will fail — not because the
  code is broken, but because the test was hard-coded to yesterday's
  count.

  It's like writing "there are 50 US states" into every test in your
  codebase, then having to update all of them when a 51st state is
  added. Better to write "however many states there are, that's the
  right count."

  Fix: instead of `expect(items).toHaveLength(27)`, write
  `expect(items).toHaveLength(options.professionalCategory.length)`.
  The test now says "however many entries are in the options file,
  the screen should render exactly that many." Adding a new option
  doesn't break the test.

YOUR CHOICES:
  A) Test against `options.professionalCategory.length` (recommended,
     future-proof).
  B) Keep hardcoded 27 (works today, breaks the day someone edits
     the JSON).

ISSUE 8 — Two behaviors on Page 9 aren't specified
------------------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT THE PLAN COVERS:
  - Islam + subsect happy path
  - Non-Islam auto-advance
  - Back-nav from Islam+subsect to Christianity (subsect resets)
  - Forward re-select same religion

WHAT'S NOT COVERED:
  1. If the user picks Islam, then picks Islam AGAIN (tapping the same
     row twice) — what happens? Nothing (no-op)? Reset the subsect?
     Assume no-op is fine but should be explicit.
  2. If the user has picked Islam + Sunni, then in the SAME session
     (no back-nav, just tapping a different religion row on the same
     screen) picks Christianity — should subsect clear? The plan only
     covers the back-nav case; this is the forward case.

WHY THIS MATTERS:
  Undefined behavior means the developer makes a call, and that call
  might not match what you'd want. Then a QA session finds it and
  we're back here.

YOUR CHOICES:
  A) Add both to the plan: same-religion re-tap is a no-op;
     different-religion-tap-on-same-screen clears subsect and (if
     non-Islam) triggers auto-advance (recommended, complete spec).
  B) Leave to developer discretion.

ISSUE 9 — Back-navigation on Page 10 (professional category)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT THE PLAN SAYS:
  Nothing about back-nav. Just "tap writes and auto-advances."

WHY THIS MATTERS:
  `professional_category` is NOT one of the fields the server treats
  as immutable (checked against `immutableFieldHelper.ts` — only
  sex, first_name, last_name, birthday, religion, subsect are locked
  server-side). So the user CAN come back to page 10 and change their
  answer.

  The behavior should be: tapping a different category on back-nav
  overwrites the previous choice in the draft, then auto-advances
  as before. Trivial to implement, but not stated.

YOUR CHOICES:
  A) Add a bullet: "On back-nav, tapping a different row overwrites
     professional_category in the draft" (recommended).
  B) Leave unstated.

ISSUE 10 — Localization gap (Urdu display of options)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT'S HAPPENING:
  The options for religion, subsect, professional category, etc. live
  as raw English strings in JSON files. When the user switches the app
  to Urdu, the tile labels stay in English.

  This matches the architecture's design (§5.3 says options are the
  literal DB values). But Phase 4 doesn't call it out — a user on
  the Urdu locale will see:
       [ اسلام ]  ← screen title in Urdu
       [ Islam ]  ← tile label still in English
       [ Christianity ]
       [ Hinduism ]

WHY THIS MATTERS:
  It's not a bug per se — it's a deliberate design choice. But if you
  weren't aware, you'd flag it as broken later. Better to confirm the
  stance now.

YOUR CHOICES:
  A) Intentional — options display in English regardless of locale.
     Ship as-is (matches architecture).
  B) Add Urdu translations for each option value (parallel maps like
     labels.ur.json). More work; deferred to a later phase.
