CONCERN #1 — BLOCKER — What happens when the user picks a photo they don't like?
----------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT THE PLAN SAYS: Page 28 shows a 6-tile grid. Tap an empty tile, the
phone's photo library opens, pick a photo, that tile is now filled with
your photo. Continue button is enabled once you have at least 1 photo.

THE PROBLEM: The plan never says what happens AFTER a tile is filled.
Can the user tap that tile again to remove the photo? To replace it?
Right now, if you followed the plan literally, once you picked a photo
you'd be stuck with it forever — no way to swap it out short of restarting
onboarding. That's obviously wrong, but the plan doesn't tell me how you
want the "delete/replace" gesture to work.

ANALOGY: Imagine you're picking clothes to wear from your wardrobe and
laying them out on your bed in 6 slots. The plan tells me how you PUT
clothes in the slots but says nothing about how you TAKE THEM OUT if you
change your mind. Do you tap the slot again? Long-press? Is there a
little "x" in the corner? Someone has to decide.

WHAT MUZZ DOES (from the reference screenshots): Filled tiles have a
small "x" button in the top-right corner. Tapping the "x" removes the
photo. Tapping the photo itself opens the picker again to swap it.

PICK ONE:
  (a) Match Muzz — small "x" in the top-right of each filled tile to
      remove; tapping the photo itself opens the picker to replace. Two
      distinct gestures, most familiar to users, recommended.
  (b) Tap-to-replace only — tapping a filled tile just re-opens the
      picker. No dedicated remove gesture (user has to replace it with
      something else, or the tile stays filled). Simpler code, worse UX.
  (c) Long-press to remove; tap to replace. Slightly hidden gesture but
      keeps the tile visually clean (no "x" chip).
  (d) Something else — describe it.

ANSWER: go with option a

CONCERN #2 — BLOCKER — What shape and quality should the photos be?
----------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT THE PLAN SAYS: Nothing about photo dimensions, aspect ratio, or
file size.

THE PROBLEM: When we open the phone's photo picker, we can either let
the user pick any photo as-is (portrait selfie, landscape sunset, square
Instagram post, whatever) OR we can force them to crop it to a fixed
shape before we accept it. And we can either take the photo at full
device resolution (huge files) or compress it (smaller, faster).

Since the 6-tile grid displays every photo at the same tile size, if
users pick a mix of portrait and landscape and square photos, the grid
will look ragged — some tiles cropped, some letterboxed, some stretched.

ANALOGY: Imagine a family photo album where every page has 6 rectangular
slots the same shape. If your family sends you a mix of tall photos,
wide photos, and square photos, the album will look messy. Either you
tell everyone "give me square photos only" (force crop) or you accept
whatever and live with the mismatched look.

WHY IT MATTERS NOW: The pictures don't get uploaded to a server yet
(that's a later phase), but they DO get displayed in the 6-tile grid
immediately. So the choice affects what you see on page 28 today.

PICK ONE:
  (a) Force square crop, medium quality (0.8) — the picker opens with a
      1:1 crop overlay, user drags to frame, we save the cropped square
      at ~80% JPEG quality. Grid always looks tidy. Muzz-style.
      Recommended.
  (b) Force portrait crop (4:5), medium quality — like Instagram feed
      photos. Slightly more space for faces.
  (c) Accept any shape, no crop, full quality — user picks whatever they
      have. Grid looks messy but nobody has to crop anything.
  (d) Something else.

ANSWER: go with option a

CONCERN #3 — BLOCKER — What does the Continue button say on page 28?
----------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT THE PLAN SAYS: The button label is "Add photos" and it's disabled
until at least 1 tile is filled.

THE PROBLEM: "Add photos" is a strange label to click AFTER you've
already added photos. It reads like an instruction, not a next-step
action. Most apps flip the label once you've done the action — e.g.
"Add photos" (0 photos) → "Continue" (1+ photos).

ANALOGY: Imagine an elevator button that says "Press here to go up".
Fine. But once you've pressed it and the elevator's on its way, the
button doesn't keep saying "Press here to go up" — it lights up and
you move on. The label should reflect where you ARE in the flow.

PICK ONE:
  (a) Label flips: "Add photos" when 0 filled → "Continue" when 1+
      filled. Muzz-style, matches how phase 9 personality button flips.
      Recommended.
  (b) Label stays "Add photos" the whole time — user just taps it once
      it's enabled. Simpler, less polished.
  (c) Something else — e.g. "Continue" the whole time, disabled when 0.

ANSWER: go with option a

CONCERN #4 — BLOCKER — What country does the phone screen default to?
----------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT THE PLAN SAYS: Page 29 has a country dial-code prefix (+92, +44,
+1, etc.) next to the phone number input. The plan says it "defaults to
the value written on page 15".

THE PROBLEM (small): Page 15 stores the user's COUNTRY (like "Pakistan"
or the code "PK"), not the DIAL CODE. So the phone screen has to look
up "PK → +92" in our country list. That's a two-step lookup, not a
direct read. No big deal — I'll wire it up. But you should know it's not
literally reading the same field.

THE PROBLEM (bigger): What if the user reaches page 29 without ever
picking a country on page 15? This can happen if they resumed onboarding
from a checkpoint that was saved BEFORE page 15, or if the draft was
partially wiped. What dial code should the picker show by default in
that case?

ANALOGY: Imagine a hotel check-in form that pre-fills your address based
on your reservation. But what if the reservation has no address? The
form has to fall back to something — maybe blank, maybe the hotel's own
country, maybe your device's country. The system needs a fallback rule.

PICK ONE:
  (a) Default to +92 (Pakistan) — since Knotify is primarily targeting
      Pakistani users, this is the safest fallback. Recommended.
  (b) Leave the picker blank and force the user to pick a country
      manually before they can type a number. Safer for non-Pakistani
      users, slightly more friction.
  (c) Read the phone's own SIM country code — most accurate but requires
      an extra permission and adds complexity.
  (d) Something else.

ANSWER: Default to +91 India, we are primarily targetting indian audience

CONCERN #6 — Heads-up — This phase needs a full app rebuild, not a JS reload
----------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT THE PLAN NEEDS: Two new native pieces —
  - expo-image-picker (opens the phone's photo library)
  - libphonenumber-js (validates phone numbers)

The first one, expo-image-picker, is a "native module". It touches
Android's operating system directly to open the photo picker and
request the READ_MEDIA_IMAGES permission. That means it's not something
we can just push through Metro (the tool that hot-reloads JavaScript
changes on your phone in seconds). It requires rebuilding the entire
Android APK and reinstalling it on your device — a 4-6 minute process.

ANALOGY: Renovating a house usually means moving furniture around
(quick — that's JavaScript changes). But if you're adding a NEW ROOM,
you need to break down walls and pour concrete (slow — that's a native
module). Photo pickers require pouring concrete.

WHAT THIS MEANS FOR YOU: When you test phase 10 for the first time on
your phone, we'll need to run the full `npx expo run:android` rebuild
sequence (per your memory: JAVA_HOME + platform-tools PATH + reverse
port forward + full APK install). Not a Metro reload. This is a
one-time cost for this phase — once installed, subsequent JS-only
tweaks reload normally.

NO ACTION NEEDED — just letting you know the rebuild is coming so
you're not surprised when I ask for a longer build window.

CONCERN #7 — Heads-up — Photos might disappear if you close the app
----------------------------------------------------------------

WHY: Photos on page 28 are stored as "local URIs" — basically, the
address on your phone where the photo lives. We don't upload them to
a server yet (that's a later phase). On Android specifically, these
addresses (they look like "content://media/external/…") are only
guaranteed to work while the app is running. If you close the app
completely and reopen it, the addresses may no longer resolve — and
the tiles would show broken/blank images.

ANALOGY: Imagine giving your friend a slip of paper with a temporary
locker number at the airport ("locker 42"). It works while they're at
the airport, but once they leave, that locker number means nothing.
Same idea — the photo address is a temporary locker number.

WHY IT'S NOT A BUG (yet): This will be fixed for real when we add the
"upload photos to backend" phase. Once photos are uploaded, we store
the permanent server URL instead of the temporary local address.

WHAT I'LL DO: Document this in the code so future-us (or a tester)
doesn't file it as a bug. The workaround for testing is: don't close
the app between picking photos and finishing onboarding.

NO ACTION NEEDED.
